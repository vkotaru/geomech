    Conventions¶

    The choices below are fixed by the code; each one has surprised someone reading an equation. Equation numbers refer to Lee et al., Global Formulations of Lagrangian and Hamiltonian Dynamics on Manifolds.

    Signs¶

    E = −(Euler–Lagrange) + forcing. compute_eom returns, for each variation vector, the coefficient E of that variation in the integrand of δS after integration by parts, and the equation is E = 0. For L(x, ẋ) and δW = δx·f this is

    E = −(d/dt ∂L/∂ẋ − ∂L/∂x) + f,

    so the point mass L = ½ m ẋ·ẋ with δW = δx·F gives E = −m ẍ + F, and the free rigid body gives E = −J Ω̇ − Ω × JΩ + M (Eq. 6.16 with every term moved to one side).

    M = −(physical inertia) in to_standard_form. The blocks split the same E: E = M a + f + G u, so M[ẍ] = −m I for the point mass, M[Ω̇] = −J for a rigid body, and G[F] = I for a force entering as δx·F.

    VBL. linearize takes δE and defines its blocks so that the physical inertia is positive: δE = −M δv̇ + A δx + B δu, i.e. M[δv̇] = −coeff(δv̇, δE), and the result reads M δẋ = A δx + B δu (Variation-Based Linearization).

    S² equations live on the tangent plane¶

    For a point q ∈ S² the variation is δq = ξ × q with ξ ⊥ q, so only the part of E orthogonal to q is an equation: (I − q qᵀ) E = 0. E as derived in general has a component along q (from the forcing, from ω × c, from A ω̇), and to_standard_form and linearize return the terms as derived, not projected. To integrate, solve (I − qqᵀ)(M ω̇ + f + Σ G u) = 0 together with ω̇ ⊥ q; the unprojected 3×3 solve gives q·ω̇ ≠ 0 and a trajectory that leaves the sphere. numeric.check_eom compares on the tangent plane for the same reason.

    Rows and columns¶

    There is one VECTOR type; a row is recognised by its structure (is_row_vector): Transpose(column), a row times a matrix vᵀM (MVMul(row, M)), a scalar multiple of a row, a sum whose first addend is a row, and d/dt, δ, ∫ of a row.

    • s * v.T() builds SVMul(vᵀ, s), printed (v)'*s; v.T() * s builds Transpose(SVMul(v, s)), printed (v*s)'. Both are the same row; simplify rewrites (s vᵀ) w to s (vᵀ w), but (vᵀ s) w and s (vᵀ w) are not merged yet.
    • v.T() * w is the inner product (VVMul, a scalar), v * w.T() the outer product (a matrix). Integration by parts works on Dot; collect turns an inner product holding the velocity of a variation vector into a Dot, so v.T() * A * v / 2 in a kinetic energy derives (tested for ½ ẋᵀAẋ and ½ ΩᵀJΩ, which gives the same string as ½ Ω·JΩ). Dot(v, A * v) is still the form the rest of the engine knows best: prefer it when there is a choice.
    • Dot, Cross and Hat take columns; Dot(a.T(), b), Cross(a, b.T()), Hat(a.T()) and a sum mixing a row and a column (a.T() + b) are still built (existing code constructs them) but check_tree rejects them.

    Zeros, numbers and folding¶

    • Zero leaves. Zero prints 0, ZeroVector prints 0v, ZeroMatrix prints 0; Number(0) prints (0) and is flagged zero. Transpose(ZeroVector) ((0v)') is the zero row that linearize uses for an empty scalar-row × vector-column block. The variation and the derivative of a Constant symbol are the zero leaves of its type (Zero, ZeroVector, ZeroMatrix; a vector or matrix of a size other than 3 or 3×3 gets a zero leaf of its own size). geomech.core.base.constants has zero_like(expr) and negate(expr) for code that builds trees.
    • 0 + x is x for every type, so sum([a, b, c]) works for vectors and matrices. x + 0 keeps the zero until simplify for a scalar (m + 0 prints (m+(0))), and is an error for a vector or a matrix (0 is a scalar: VAdd cannot combine VECTOR 'x' and SCALAR '(0)'); write x + ZeroVector or leave the zero out.
    • Products. Every product node prints its two factors joined by *: Mul (m*l), SVMul (x*m), SMMul (J*(2)), MVMul (J*\Omega), MMMul (R*J), VVMul ((a)'*b, a*(b)'). Before phase 5b the factors were juxtaposed, so Mul(ab, c) and Mul(a, bc) both printed abc and compared equal. repr keeps · between factors and * as the constant marker.
    • Numbers. Number(2.5) prints (2.5), Number(-1) (-1); x * -1 and -x are SVMul(x, (-1)), printed x*(-1). A scalar factor is always normalised to the right of SVMul/SMMul (m * x is SVMul(x, m), printed x*m). simplify multiplies the numeric factors of a product chain into one number placed last (Mul(-0.5, Mul(m, 2)) → Mul(m, -1), (-1)*m*l*l → m*l*l*(-1)); a float with an integer value becomes an int (0.5 · 2 · (-1) prints (-1), not (-1.0), and a bare Number(2.0) simplifies to (2)), and every number literal is written the way Number writes it (Scalar("0.5", value=0.5) simplifies to (0.5)), so every equation prints its coefficients the same way. Numeric addends are summed.
    • Like terms. Two addends merge when, after flattening their products, they have the same vector or matrix base and the same symbolic factors as a multiset; their numbers are added: x*(2)*y + x*y*(3) → x*y*(5), v a − v a → 0, (vᵀw)s + s(vᵀw) → (vᵀw)*s*(2) ((vᵀ s) w is first pulled to (vᵀ w) s). Addends with different symbolic coefficients are not factored (x a + x b stays). Scalar products, Dot and the inner product of two columns are treated as commutative (aᵀb s − bᵀa s is 0); bases and factors built with MMMul, MVMul, Cross, SVMul, SMMul or an outer product are compared operand by operand, so A B v s − B A v s and (a×b) m + (b×a) m are not merged. Grouping is one pass, keyed by geomech.core.transformations.simplification.term_key.
    • Float cancellation. Like terms are merged by their numeric coefficients; a sum whose magnitude is at most 1e-12 times the largest coefficient that went into it is treated as an exact zero (_CANCEL_RTOL in simplification.py): x*0.1*3 − x*0.3 is 0, not x(5.55e-17).
    • A named number. Scalar("(-1)") without value prints exactly like Number(-1) but is a symbol; check_tree rejects it. Scalar("m", value=2) is a symbol with a value: every simplification keeps it by name (m + m is m*(2), never (4); with l = Scalar("l", value=3), m·l stays m*l, never (6)), to_latex prints m, and only geomech.numeric (Evaluator, evaluate, check_eom) uses the 2. A number literal is a scalar leaf printed as its own value: Number(2), Zero, One, Scalar("0.5", value=0.5) and the numbers the operators wrap (2 * x); geomech.core.base.expressions.is_number_literal tells them apart.

    str is the identity¶

    a == b is type(a) is type(b) and str(a) == str(b), plus, for a calculus node (Variation, TimeDerivative, TimeIntegral), the classes of the nodes down to its first non-calculus operand; hashing uses str. expr.has(x) uses the same ==. Hence:

    • two leaves with the same printed name are the same symbol, whatever their flags (Scalar("k", attr=["Constant"]) == Scalar("k")), so J2 = Matrix("J1") silently merges two inertias, and two S2("q") objects are the same point;
    • leaves of different classes differ even with one name: Scalar("x") != Vector("x"), Variation(Scalar("x")) != Variation(Vector("x")), Transpose(Vector("x")) != Transpose(Matrix("x")), and Scalar("m") does not occur in Dot(Vector("m"), w);
    • products print a * between their factors, and * is reserved for that: a symbol name may not contain it (Scalar("m*l") raises ValueError; write ml, or x^\ast for a superscript star). Every * in a printed form is therefore a product boundary, so two products print alike only when they multiply factors that print alike, in the same order: Mul(ab, c) is ab*c and Mul(a, bc) is a*bc, but the regroupings (a*b)*c and a*(b*c) both print a*b*c and are equal (they have one value);
    • commutative rearrangements are different trees (a*b vs b*a), so like-term merging uses a structural key (term_key) that knows Dot, Mul and inner products commute, and a few equivalent terms are not merged;
    • nodes are frozen dataclasses with tuple operands: a tree cannot change after it is built (assignment raises FrozenInstanceError), so a node can be shared between trees and used as a dictionary key; pickling and copy.deepcopy work;
    • the pinned tests compare str of every equation byte for byte. repr is the readable debugging form (leaf kinds v:x, M:J, S2:q, SO3:R, a trailing * for a constant, · for products) and is not an identity.

    Glossary¶

    • configuration variable: A leaf listed in SystemVariables: a Scalar, a Vector, an S2 point or an SO3 point. Hamilton's principle varies it; each one gets an equation.
    • tangent vector: The velocity coordinate of a manifold point: ω_q for S2("q") (q̇ = ω × q, ω ⊥ q) and Ω_R for SO3("R") (Ṙ = R Hat(Ω), body frame). Obtained with q.get_tangent_vector(); a TS2/TSO3 leaf that remembers its point.
    • variation vector: The free variation of a configuration variable, whose coefficient is the equation: δx for a Scalar/Vector, ξ_q for S2 (δq = ξ × q, ξ ⊥ q) and η_R for SO3 (δR = R Hat(η)). The keys of the compute_eom result are the printed variation vectors. Used on the EOM side.
    • perturbation: A variable of the linearization (VBL side): the configuration perturbations (δx, ξ, η), the velocity perturbations (δẋ, δω, δΩ), their derivatives and the input variations δu. They are the columns of M, A, B.
    • params: Prescribed time-varying symbols declared with compute_eom(..., params=[l]): their rate (l̇) appears in the equations, their variation is not free and is dropped. to_standard_form accepts them; linearize does not yet.
    • inputs: The forces and torques passed to to_standard_form/linearize (inputs=[f, M]): G (standard form) and B (linearization) are their coefficients. For compute_eom they are ordinary non-constant symbols in δW. An input must be the same symbol as its leaf in the equations (class, attr, size): Scalar("g") against a constant Scalar("g", attr=["Constant"]) raises ValueError, as does a param of compute_eom. Both functions, and StateLayout, accept and reject the same inputs (check_inputs): an ordered sequence (a list, tuple or generator) of distinct non-constant Scalar/Vector leaves that are neither a configuration variable, a tangent or variation vector (ω, ξ, Ω, η), nor printed like a velocity, an acceleration or any other node of the equations; inputs=None means none. A set or a dict raises TypeError: the order of inputs is the order of the columns of B and of the generated u vector, and a set has no order (it would depend on the hash seed). A size may be spelled 3, [3] or (3,): leaves store it as a tuple. A param of compute_eom may also be an input only while its rate does not occur in the equations (AlgebraicError otherwise).
    • constant: A leaf with attr=["Constant"] (or a number): its variation and derivative vanish. A symbol that is neither a configuration variable, a param nor a constant makes compute_eom raise (Troubleshooting).
