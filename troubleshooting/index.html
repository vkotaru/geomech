    Troubleshooting¶

    Three kinds of trouble: the equation is wrong (or looks odd) and nothing was raised; a typed error was raised; or you want to see what the engine did. The tools are listed at the end.

    The equation looks wrong and nothing was raised¶

    These can happen with the current version. Bugs that used to give a plausible wrong equation silently, and are fixed, are listed separately under History so that an old notebook or an old result can be recognised; they are not suspects for a wrong equation today.

    Symptom Cause Fix
    A δm/ṁ term, or a ValueError "δS still contains the variation symbols '\delta{m}'" m is not Constant Scalar("m", attr=["Constant"]); attribute names are case-sensitive
    q × (ω̇ × q) stays in an S² equation ω̇ ⊥ q holds on the sphere but is never assumed: the tangency test sees ω, ξ and Cross(·, q) only, not a d/dt (vector_rules reaches every node, so ω·q and q × (ω × q) are reduced wherever they sit) correct but unreduced; to_standard_form reads it as the projection (I − qqᵀ) ω̇
    Partially simplified output, with a RuntimeWarning "no confirmed fixed point after N passes" full_simplify stopped at max_iter raise max_iter, or look at the expression in the warning
    Two different inertias print the same and are merged Two leaves with the same name are the same symbol (str is the identity): J2 = Matrix("J1") give every symbol its own name; print_tree shows the names
    On S², to_standard_form's 3×3 solve leaves the sphere E is meaningful on the tangent plane only solve (I − qqᵀ)(M ω̇ + f + G u) = 0 with ω̇ ⊥ q (Conventions)
    M is negative E = −(Euler–Lagrange), so M = −(physical inertia) by convention (Conventions)
    A wrong sign, a term missing, an extra term, and no row here matches an engine rule that rewrites a term wrongly the recipe below

    The equation is wrong and no row matches¶

    First confirm it numerically: check_eom(L, dW, variables, eom) compares the equation with a finite-difference Euler–Lagrange residual computed without the library's rules (Verifying an Equation Numerically); a relative error far above 1e-5 means a wrong term. Then localise it in three steps.

    1. Bisect with summary(). Keep only the terms of L (and dW) that the wrong term can come from, run compute_eom(..., trace=tr) with tr = EOMTrace(), and read tr.summary() against the formulas of Pipeline: the first stage that disagrees (a substitution that is not ξ̇ − 2 ω × ξ, a symbol that disappears, a node count that drops sharply) is the stage to suspect. tr.ibp[t]["collect"], ["integrate"] and ["simplify"] split integration by parts per target.
    2. Run the engine fuzzer. From the repository root, python scripts/engine_fuzz.py --quick applies every rewrite (expand, pull, vector_rules, simplify, collect, the manifold rules, the product rules) to thousands of random trees and compares input and output numerically. A rule that changes a value is reported as a [wrong-value] finding with a shrunk counterexample naming the transformation (manifold_rules[full delta], collect[target occurs once], vector_rules: z_v Dot(q, ω_p) → z_v 0); a correct engine has none. No hand derivation is needed.
    3. Rerun the suspect stage under set_trace. geomech.set_trace(True), then call the stage on the previous stage of the trace by hand, for example full_simplify(tr["manifold_rules"]) or collect(tr["ibp.prepare"], target). Each line is rule name: before ==> after; the module docstring lists every rule with its identity and the hypothesis it needs, so a line that is false on sight, or a rule that fired without its hypothesis, is the bug.

    The AlgebraicErrors about a calculus operator¶

    All three end with the engine text "appears only inside the calculus operator … such a term has no linear coefficient", but they have unrelated causes; the first sentence tells them apart. compute_eom decides between the first and the third by the order of the surviving derivative and by looking for an acceleration in L, before it says anything about collect.

    1. A d/dt of a variation vector survived integration by parts (from compute_eom). The derivative is of first order and L holds no acceleration, but collect could not bring its term into the shape Dot(d/dt(η), f) (for a scalar variable: a direct factor d/dt(δs) g of a product), so the IBP pass did not integrate it, and the coefficient of η cannot be read off:

    AlgebraicError: compute_eom: while extracting the equation for '\eta_{R}': the time
derivative '\frac{d}{dt}(\eta_{R})' of the variation vector '\eta_{R}' survived integration
by parts in the term 'Dot(w,Vee(Hat(\frac{d}{dt}(\eta_{R}))*A))'
of δS; collect could not expose it as the left operand of a Dot (Dot(\frac{d}{dt}(\eta_{R}), f)),
so the term was not integrated by parts and has no coefficient. Inspect the term with
print_tree(term, style='indent'), or run compute_eom(..., trace=EOMTrace()) and look at the
stage 'ibp.collect[\frac{d}{dt}(\eta_{R})]'. (engine: extract_linear_coeff: '\eta_{R}' appears
only inside the calculus operator '\frac{d}{dt}(\eta_{R})' (a higher order derivative, a
variation or an integral of the query); such a term has no linear coefficient)

    Fix: rewrite the term so the velocity sits in a vector operand (Dot(w, Vee(Hat(Ω) A)) above: express the Vee explicitly, or use an equivalent Dot/Cross form). A row form v.T() * A * w in a kinetic energy is handled (collect turns an inner product holding the target into a Dot); if one still survives, the message adds "write it as Dot(v, A * w)".

    2. A jerk (from to_standard_form): an acceleration appears only under a further time derivative, so the equation is not second order:

    AlgebraicError: to_standard_form: in equation '\delta{xs}', term
'\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{d}{dt}(xs)))': the acceleration
'\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{d}{dt}(xs))' occurs only inside
'\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{d}{dt}(xs)))', a third or higher time derivative of the
configuration (a jerk): the equation is not second order, so it has no standard form
M a + f + G u = 0 (engine: extract_linear_coeff: '\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{d}{dt}(xs))' appears
only inside the calculus operator '\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{d}{dt}(xs)))' (a higher
order derivative, a variation or an integral of the query); such a term has no linear
coefficient)

    Fix: a jerk comes from a virtual work that holds a third time derivative of the configuration; compute_eom output is fine, only the standard form does not exist.

    3. A Lagrangian in accelerations (from compute_eom): L holds ẍ (or ω̇, Ω̇), so δL holds d/dt(d/dt(δx)). Integration by parts runs once per variation vector, against its first derivative, so the second derivative survives. For L = ½ ẍ·ẍ:

    AlgebraicError: compute_eom: while extracting the equation for '\delta{x}': the Lagrangian
depends on accelerations (L contains '\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{d}{dt}(x))'); higher-order
Lagrangians are not supported. Integration by parts runs once per variation vector, against
its first time derivative '\frac{d}{dt}(\delta{x})', so the term
'Dot(\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{d}{dt}(\delta{x})),\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{d}{dt}(x)))' of δS keeps
'\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{d}{dt}(\delta{x}))' and has no coefficient. Write L in the configuration
variables and their velocities only; run compute_eom(..., trace=EOMTrace()) and look at the
stage 'dL' to see where the acceleration enters. (engine: extract_linear_coeff: '\delta{x}'
appears only inside the calculus operator '\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{d}{dt}(\delta{x}))' (a higher
order derivative, a variation or an integral of the query); such a term has no linear
coefficient)

    Fix: higher-order Lagrangians are not supported; write L in the configuration variables and their velocities.

    check_tree(expr) raises InvariantError naming the invariant, the path from the root (L/R for binary nodes, the index for sums, x for a unary operand) and the subtree:

    Tools¶

    • compute_eom(..., trace=EOMTrace()) keeps every stage (also after an exception: tr.failed_at then names the stage that raised, and tr.summary() starts with FAILED at <stage>: <exception>); tr.summary(), tr.show("simplify"). A trace is cleared when a pipeline starts, so reusing one object keeps only the last run. linearize(..., trace=LinearizationTrace()) keeps δE, the term buckets, the raw and the final blocks per row. See Pipeline.
    • GEOMECH_DEBUG=1 (environment variable, read at import): one DEBUG line per pipeline stage on the geomech logger (stage, node count, start of the repr) and check_tree on the inputs and outputs of compute_eom, to_standard_form and linearize. In a notebook, logging.basicConfig(level=logging.DEBUG) gives the same lines without the checks (call it before set_trace(True); geomech adds its own stderr handler only when no handler is configured, so each line prints once).
    • GEOMECH_TRACE=1 or geomech.set_trace(True): every firing of a named rewrite rule, name: before ==> after, on the geomech.rules logger. The rule names, with the hypothesis each needs, are in the docstrings of the transformation modules (Pipeline).
    • print_tree(expr) (default style="indent"): one node per line with class names, L/R/index labels, every flag of each leaf and, for a tangent or variation vector, its manifold point (of=S2 'q'); two different points with one name are numbered #1, #2. repr(expr) marks constants with * and the leaf kind (v:x, M:J, S2:q).
    • check_tree(expr): the structural invariants of a hand-built or mutated tree.
    • check_eom(L, dW, variables, eom) / assert_eom: compare a derived equation with a finite-difference Euler–Lagrange residual computed without the library's rules (Verifying an Equation Numerically). A wrong term shows up as a relative error far above 1e-5.
    • scripts/engine_fuzz.py --quick: checks every rewrite numerically on random trees; a [wrong-value] finding names the transformation and a minimal counterexample (step 2 of the recipe).
    • The modules are importable for breakpoints and patches under names distinct from their functions: import geomech.core.transformations.simplification as m gives the module (expansion, pulling, vector_identities, simplification, geomech.core.math.collecting), while from geomech.core.transformations import simplify gives the function.
