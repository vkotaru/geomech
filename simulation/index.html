    Simulation¶

    geomech.simulation integrates the modules written by geomech.codegen forward in time on the manifold, and designs LQR gains from a generated linearization. It calls only the generated functions (mass, bias, input_matrix, kinematics, energy; M_lin, A_lin, B_lin), never the expression trees. The integrators are also an independent check of the derived equations: conserved quantities stay conserved, and the trajectories agree with closed forms written directly in NumPy.

    from geomech.simulation import accelerations, simulate, lqr

a = accelerations(module, state, u, p)              # mass @ a + bias + G @ u = 0, S² on T_qS²
traj = simulate(module, x0, (0.0, 10.0), p, u=None, method="rkmk4", dt=1e-3)
traj.t, traj.state, traj.constraint_drift, traj.energy()
traj.u                                              # inputs at the stored states, (N, n_inputs)
ctrl = lqr(lin_module, x_eq, u_eq, p, Q, R, module=module)   # checks (x_eq, u_eq)
traj = simulate(module, x0, 5.0, p, u=ctrl)
more = simulate(module, traj.state[-1], (5.0, 10.0), p)      # continue from the last state

    traj.u holds the inputs at the stored states (zeros for u=None), recorded during the run: with RKMK4 they are the values the first stage of each stored step used, so a controller is never called twice at one state (with solve_ivp, whose stored states are interpolated, it is called once more at each of them after the run). An exception raised by a controller propagates as soon as it is raised, unchanged, with a note giving the time. geomech.viz.Report reads traj.u to label a forced run's energy as "energy change" rather than drift; a hand-built Trajectory without u gets the neutral "energy change … inputs not recorded".

    scipy is an optional dependency (pip install "geomech[sim]"), imported only by method="solve_ivp" and by lqr; import geomech.simulation does not import it, and method="rkmk4" needs only NumPy.

    The state¶

    The state is the flat array of the module's LAYOUT (see the state layout): every configuration slot in variable order, then every velocity slot.

    Variable Configuration Velocity Constraint
    s: Scalar s (1) ṡ (1)
    x: Vector x (3) ẋ (3)
    q: S2 q (3) ω (3) ‖q‖ = 1, q · ω = 0 (q̇ = ω × q)
    R: SO3 R (9, row-major) Ω (3, body frame) RᵀR = I, det R = 1 (Ṙ = R Ω̂)

    simulate checks x0 against these constraints (tolerance 1e-9) and raises ValueError naming the slot, for example x0: the S2 slot 'q' must have unit norm, got |q| = 1.01 or x0: the velocity slot '\omega_{q}' must be orthogonal to 'q'. check_state(module, state, tol) runs the same check. A trajectory gives the values of a slot by its printed symbol or its generated name: traj["q"] is (N, 3), traj["R"] is (N, 3, 3), traj["omega_q"] is (N, 3). The StateLayout the module was generated from builds a state from named slots and splits one (pack/unpack):

    x0 = layout.pack(q=q0, omega_q=w0)          # velocities left out are zero
layout.unpack(traj.state[-1])["omega_q"]    # (3,)

    Every stored state is on the manifold (see the two integrators below), so traj.state[-1] passes the check and can start the next segment, for example to switch controllers.

    Accelerations and the S² solve¶

    The generated equations read mass @ a + bias + input_matrix @ u = 0 with M = −(physical inertia) (Conventions). For R, R³ and SO(3) rows the blocks are square and invertible for a physical system.

    An S² equation is meaningful only on the tangent plane of its point (Conventions), and the derived mass block may be singular or not:

    System S² mass block Rank
    quadrotor with a suspended load Hat(q) Hat(q) m_Q l² = −m_Q l² (I − qqᵀ) 2
    spherical pendulum −m l² I (the engine already used q · ω̇ = 0) 3

    In both cases the unprojected 3×3 solve is not the equation of motion: it either fails or gives q · ω̇ ≠ 0, and the trajectory leaves the sphere. The missing equation is the tangency of the acceleration: differentiating q · ω = 0 gives (ω × q) · ω + q · ω̇ = 0, and the first term vanishes, so q · ω̇ = 0. accelerations replaces each S² row block (all its columns, including couplings to other variables' accelerations) by

    P_q · row  +  s q qᵀ on its own ω̇ column,      right-hand side  −P_q (bias + G u),

    with P_q = I − q qᵀ. Multiplying the new rows by qᵀ gives q · ω̇ = 0; multiplying by P_q gives the tangent-plane equation. The test suite checks both (to 1e-12 and 1e-10) on every S² system of the oracle gate, including the two of the table.

    Scaling. The result must not depend on the units. The stabiliser weight is s = ‖P_q D‖_F / √2, with D the row's own diagonal mass block. Both forms in the table project to P_q D = −m l² P_q, and ‖P_q‖_F = √2, so s = m l²: the normal direction gets the magnitude of the two tangent directions (for an anisotropic tangent inertia, their root mean square). Then every row of the system and its right-hand side is divided by the row's largest entry (row equilibration) and the system is solved with np.linalg.solve. The solution is the same; the rounding and the condition number no longer depend on how the masses and lengths compare with 1. With a unit stabiliser and no equilibration, a double spherical pendulum with m₂/m₁ = 1e-9 lost 8 digits silently, and a 1 t mass on a 100 km tether (SI units, m l² = 1e13) was rejected as singular. Both are now solved to round-off (condition numbers 2.9 and 1.1).

    A singular system (condition number above 1e12 after equilibration: a zero mass, a singular inertia, a kinetic energy that ignores a velocity such as ½ m |ẋ + l q̇|², condition number ≈ 4e16) raises np.linalg.LinAlgError with the condition number and the state. accelerations checks the condition number on every call; simulate checks it at x0 and afterwards only catches a failed or non-finite solve.

    Integrators¶

    method="rkmk4" (default): Runge–Kutta–Munthe-Kaas of order 4¶

    A fixed-step Lie-group integrator. The state is moved by a group element exp(U) of the product group that acts on it:

    • each S² point by a rotation from the left, q ← expm(u) q (so q̇ = ω × q has generator ω);
    • each SO(3) point by a rotation from the right, R ← R expm(u) (generator Ω);
    • every other slot (R, R³ configuration and all velocities) by a translation.

    One step of size h applies classical RK4 to the ODE of U in the Lie algebra, U̇ = dexp⁻¹_U(A(exp(U)·yₙ)), U(tₙ) = 0 (Munthe-Kaas, High order Runge–Kutta methods on manifolds, Appl. Numer. Math. 29, 1999; Hairer, Lubich, Wanner, Geometric Numerical Integration, §IV.8):

    K₁ = h F(0),   K₂ = h F(K₁/2),   K₃ = h F(K₂/2),   K₄ = h F(K₃),
U = (K₁ + 2K₂ + 2K₃ + K₄) / 6,   yₙ₊₁ = exp(U) · yₙ,
F(V) = dexp⁻¹_V(A(exp(V) · yₙ))    at t, t + h/2, t + h/2, t + h.

    dexp⁻¹_V(G) = G − ½[V, G] + 1/12 [V, [V, G]] + … is truncated after the ad² term. On so(3) ≅ R³ the bracket is the cross product; for the right action on SO(3) the sign of the odd term flips (dexp⁻¹_{−V}); on the vector slots every bracket vanishes and the step is exactly classical RK4. Because V = O(h) and the ad³ coefficient is zero, the truncation changes each Kᵢ by O(h⁵) and the method keeps order 4. Without the brackets the method drops to order 2 on a tumbling rigid body (error ratio 4.0 per halving instead of 16).

    q and R are only ever multiplied by rotation matrices, so |q| − 1 and ‖RᵀR − I‖ stay at round-off (≈ 5e-14 over 10 s) without projection. q · ω = 0 is a velocity constraint that the dynamics preserve (d/dt (q·ω) = 0 from the S² solve), but ω is a vector slot that is translated while q is rotated, so the integrator alone would keep it only to its truncation error. After each step ω ← ω − (q·ω) q, a change of the size of the local error that keeps order 4, so q · ω stays at round-off too.

    dt is shortened, if needed, so that it divides tf − t0. The trajectory holds every step, or every output_every-th step (and the last) with simulate(..., output_every=k); the integration is the same, only the storage is thinned (1000 s of the quadrotor with a load at dt = 1e-3 is about 190 MB in full).

    method="solve_ivp"¶

    scipy.integrate.solve_ivp (ivp_method="DOP853" by default, rtol/atol passed through) on the embedded state with ẏ = (kinematics(y), accelerations(y)). The solver does not know the manifold, so the outputs (at spacing dt, thinned by output_every) are projected onto it: q/|q|, ω made tangent (ω − (q·ω) q), and the polar factor of R (U Vᵀ of its SVD). traj.constraint_drift reports the drift before projection: |q|−1 reaches about 3e-10 over 10 s at rtol = 1e-10.

    traj.state, and so traj.energy(), traj["q"] and the controller errors computed from it, are the projected states. At rtol = 1e-10 projection changes the energy by about 1e-10, below every tolerance of the table below; at a loose rtol it hides part of the drift, so check traj.constraint_drift as well.

    Inputs¶

    u is None (zero inputs), a constant 1-D array of LAYOUT["n_inputs"] entries (a scalar for one input), or a callable u(t, state) evaluated at every stage (an LQRController is one). A value of another shape, such as a (2, 2) array for four inputs, raises ValueError rather than being flattened; so do non-finite entries (u has non-finite entries at [3], with the time for a callable). A callable that returns None, typically a controller missing its return, raises TypeError naming the time instead of running open loop.

    A callable u must be a pure function of (t, state). RKMK4 evaluates it at the stage states (t + h/2 twice, at two different states), and solve_ivp at trial steps that it may reject and at times that are not monotone. A controller with internal state (an integral term, a filter, a log) must not update that state inside u; integrate it as part of the model instead, or update it between simulate calls of one step each.

    Energy and momentum checks¶

    traj.energy() evaluates the module's energy(state, p) (generated with generate_module(..., lagrangian=L), see Total energy) at every output state; traj.energy(fn) uses another function fn(state, p), such as energy_function(L, layout).

    tests/test_simulation_conservation.py runs the systems of tests/vbl_systems.py for 10 s with zero inputs from the states of tests/simulation_systems.py. Relative energy drift, max |E(t) − E(0)| / |E(0)|:

    System Tolerance rkmk4, dt = 1e-3 solve_ivp, rtol = 1e-10
    spherical pendulum 1e-8 4.0e-13 6.5e-10
    free rigid body 1e-9 3.0e-15 3.7e-11
    rigid pendulum about pivot 1e-8 2.8e-14 4.8e-10
    double rigid pendulum 1e-7 1.9e-14 9.4e-11
    elastic spherical pendulum 1e-7 3.4e-12 8.5e-10
    quadrotor with suspended load 1e-7 2.6e-11 5.2e-11

    The inertial angular momentum R J Ω of the free rigid body is conserved to 1e-9 by both methods. Two closed forms, written in NumPy without geomech and integrated with DOP853 at rtol = 1e-13, agree with both methods to 1e-8 over 2 s: the spherical pendulum (Lee et al. Eq. 5.14, m l² ω̇ = m g l e₃ × q for the potential m g l e₃·q, q̇ = ω × q, in R³ × R³) and the free rigid body (Euler's equations J Ω̇ = J Ω × Ω, Ṙ = R Ω̂).

    LQR from the linearization¶

    lqr(lin_module, x_eq, u_eq, p, Q, R, a_eq=None, module=None) evaluates the generated M_lin, A_lin, B_lin (Variation-Based Linearization) at an equilibrium, forms Ā = M⁻¹A, B̄ = M⁻¹B in reduced coordinates, solves the continuous algebraic Riccati equation (scipy.linalg.solve_continuous_are) and returns an LQRController with the gain K, the Riccati solution P, the reduced A, B, the closed-loop eigenvalues and the state error. Calling the controller, ctrl(t, state) = u_eq − K δx(state), is the input signature of simulate.

    One layout for both modules. Generate the standard-form and the linearization module from the same StateLayout, built with the same SystemVariables and the same inputs list (same order) as to_standard_form and linearize; build x_eq and x0 with layout.pack:

    V = SystemVariables(vectors=[x_L, q], matrices=[R])
eom = compute_eom(L, dW, V)
layout = StateLayout(V, inputs=[f, M], params=collect_params([L, dW], V, [f, M]))
plant = load_module(generate_module(to_standard_form(eom, V, inputs=[f, M]), layout,
                                    lagrangian=L))
lin_module = load_module(generate_module(linearize(eom, V, inputs=[f, M]), layout))
x_eq = layout.pack(x_L=np.zeros(3), q=[0, 0, -1], R=np.eye(3))   # hover, at rest
u_eq = np.r_[(p["m_Q"] + p["m_L"]) * p["g"], 0, 0, 0]              # [f, M]
ctrl = lqr(lin_module, x_eq, u_eq, p, Q=1.0, R=1.0, module=plant)

    generate_module raises ValueError for a linearization whose variables or inputs are in another order than the layout's (the columns of B_lin would otherwise pair with the wrong entries of u, and the loop diverges under a gain whose eigenvalues look stable), and lqr checks that LINEARIZATION matches its LAYOUT and, with module=, that the two modules have the same variables and inputs in the same order.

    The equilibrium is checked when module= is given. The linearization module has no residual, so by itself lqr cannot tell an equilibrium from any other point, and at a non-equilibrium it still returns a gain whose closed-loop eigenvalues look stable: for the rigid pendulum 30° from upright (Ω̇ = (2.46, −0.12, 0.008) there) the gain's eigenvalues have real parts ≤ −1.37, and the nonlinear loop settles 0.22 rad away from x_eq with a constant input. Pass the standard-form module of the same system (module=plant) and lqr requires

    • every velocity of x_eq to be zero (to tol; the S² reduction assumes ω_eq = 0),
    • the equation residual r = mass·a_eq + bias + G u_eq (S² rows projected) to satisfy ‖r‖∞ ≤ eq_tol · scale (eq_tol = 1e-8), where scale is the largest of ‖bias‖∞, ‖G u_eq‖∞ and the linearization's ‖A_lin T‖∞. The last term matters at an equilibrium held by a potential, where the bias itself is zero.

    Otherwise it raises ValueError with the residual and accelerations(module, x_eq, u_eq, p). Without module=, lqr emits a UserWarning that the equilibrium was not checked (an error under -W error).

    The state error uses the perturbation coordinates of linearize:

    Variable Configuration error Velocity error
    R, R³ x − x_eq ẋ − ẋ_eq
    SO(3) η = vee(log(R_eqᵀ R)) (geomech.numeric.log_so3) Ω − Ω_eq
    S² Bᵀ (q_eq × q) (2 entries) Bᵀ (ω − ω_eq) (2 entries)

    The SO(3) error follows from δR = R η̂: R = R_eq exp(η̂). On S², δq = ξ × q with ξ ⊥ q, so ξ = q_eq × q to first order.

    The S² tangent basis. The linearization of an S² point has 3-D perturbations ξ and δω, but at an equilibrium (where ω = 0) both are orthogonal to q: the normal direction is a zero mode that no input reaches, so the Riccati equation has no stabilising solution in 3-D. lqr therefore works in the 2-D basis B = tangent_basis(q_eq) (orthonormal, b₁ × b₂ = q_eq): with T block diagonal (B for each S² perturbation, I elsewhere), it uses Tᵀ M T, Tᵀ A T, Tᵀ B, so the S² rows are projected onto the tangent plane like the S² solve above. A singular S² block of M_lin (Hat(q) Hat(q) m l² for the quadrotor with a load, where the full M_lin has rank 17 of 18) becomes invertible. Q is given in the reduced coordinates (ctrl.labels names them: xi_q[0], xi_q[1], eta_R[0], …), as a scalar, a diagonal or a matrix.

    Anisotropic weights on an S² block depend on the basis. xi_q[0] and xi_q[1] are the components along b₁ and b₂ of tangent_basis(q_eq), and b₁ is the coordinate axis least aligned with q_eq, projected. A tiny change of q_eq can switch that axis and turn the basis by 90° (near q_eq = −e₃, where the two smallest components tie: (2e-9, 1e-9, −1) gives b₁ = e₂ instead of e₁), so different weights on the two entries of an S² block weight different physical directions. Give each S² block (both xi_q entries, and both delta_omega_q entries) equal weights, or check ctrl.bases[str(q)] for the basis that was used. A scalar Q is always safe.

    Example: the inverted rigid pendulum¶

    examples/inverted_rigid_pendulum_lqr.py derives a rigid pendulum about its pivot on SO(3) (Lee et al. Eq. 6.8), generates its standard form and linearization, designs an LQR gain about the inverted equilibrium R_eq = diag(1, −1, −1) and simulates the closed loop from 20° off about a random axis:

    closed loop   : [-158.082  -11.554  -11.554   -3.213   -3.213   -3.163]

open loop: |eta| goes from 20.0 deg to 137.0 deg in 1 s
closed loop:
  t = 0.0 s   |eta| = 3.491e-01 rad
  t = 1.0 s   |eta| = 1.545e-02 rad
  t = 2.0 s   |eta| = 6.498e-04 rad
  t = 5.0 s   |eta| = 4.846e-08 rad
max |RᵀR − I| over the run: 1.9e-14

    The tests also stabilise an inverted spherical pendulum (q 15° from +e₃, the maximum of m g l e₃·q, with its tangent input) to |q − e₃| < 1e-4 in 5 s, and the quadrotor with a suspended load about hover.

    Not covered¶

    Contact, constraints beyond the manifold, variable-step Lie-group integrators, MPC and real-time performance. Each RKMK4 stage costs one call of mass, bias and input_matrix plus a small dense solve: 10 s at dt = 1e-3 (40 000 stages) takes about 4.6 s for the double rigid pendulum, whose generated blocks are the slowest, and less for the single-body systems. traj.energy() calls the generated energy once per stored state (about 0.23 s for those 10 001 states, 5 % of the run), so it is not vectorised.
